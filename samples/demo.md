# The Longitude Problem

In 1714, the British Parliament offered £20,000 to anyone who could solve it.

## A Clock That Would Not Stop

Latitude was easy. The sun told you. Longitude was different — it required
knowing, precisely, what time it was somewhere else. And no clock at sea kept
time. The pitch of the ship, the salt in the air, the swing from tropics to
ice: every pendulum ever built surrendered to them.

John Harrison was a carpenter. He had no training in horology, no patron, and
no particular reason to believe he could succeed where the Royal Society had
failed. He spent forty-three years on the problem.

> The sea is not a laboratory. It is an argument with everything you have built.

His fourth attempt, H4, was the size of a pocket watch. On a voyage to Jamaica
in 1761, it lost five seconds in eighty-one days.

The Board of Longitude refused to pay him.
